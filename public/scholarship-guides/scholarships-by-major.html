How to find scholarships by college major

A major-specific scholarship search starts with your college department, then expands to professional associations, employers, research programs, and related fields. Search more than the exact name of your degree.

Some awards require an officially declared major; others accept students who plan to enter a field. Check each sponsor's wording.

Start with your college or intended department

Check the scholarship page for the college and academic department you hope to join. Departmental awards may be limited to majors, concentrations, class years, research groups, or students enrolled in a specific program. Ask the department's advising office whether applications are automatic, nomination-based, or submitted separately.

If you have not chosen a college yet, review the financial-aid and departmental scholarship pages at each institution on your list. An award may only be available after admission or after you declare the major.

Search professional associations and industry groups

Professional associations, trade organizations, employers, and charitable foundations sometimes offer scholarships related to an academic or career field. Search the national association and regional chapters. Also check whether an organization has a student membership, conference, mentoring, or scholarship program.

For example, students interested in engineering can look at engineering societies and industry groups; nursing students can check nursing and healthcare education organizations; education students can check teacher-preparation and educator associations. These are search directions, not a claim that any specific organization currently has an open award.

Use related keywords, not only the major title

A scholarship may be listed under a broader discipline, a career goal, or a related activity. Try several terms for the same field:

Look beyond department awards

  • Research programs: Some institutions support undergraduates working with faculty on funded research.
  • Employers: Companies and local businesses may fund education in fields connected to their workforce needs.
  • Community foundations: Local awards may combine a geographic requirement with a planned major or career pathway.
  • Professional and trade associations: Check the student resources and scholarship sections of organizations connected to your field.
  • College-access and career programs: Ask counselors and academic advisors about opportunities they recommend for students entering your discipline.

Check the exact major requirement

Read whether the scholarship accepts a planned major, a declared major, a particular degree level, a specific accreditation, or enrollment in a named program. Some awards require students to remain in the field, complete an internship, or meet academic-progress rules to renew the award. Do not assume that a related major qualifies unless the sponsor confirms it.
