Scholarships and financial aid for Texas students

Texas students can look for funding through colleges, state programs, local foundations, employers, community organizations, and national scholarship sponsors. The strongest search combines several sources.

State grants and privately funded scholarships are different kinds of aid. Every program has its own residency, school, enrollment, financial-need, and application rules.

Start with the college you plan to attend

Check the financial-aid office and scholarship pages for each Texas college on your list. Look for awards tied to admission, a particular department, transfer status, academic performance, financial need, service, or a specific program of study. Ask whether a separate application is required and how outside awards affect your aid package.

Also check the community college, technical institute, or university route you are considering. Different state programs are limited to different types of institutions and students.

Know the main Texas state-aid programs

The Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board publishes current program descriptions and annual guidance. Examples include:

  • TEXAS Grant: a state grant for students who meet the program's eligibility pathways and attend an eligible Texas public institution. Students must satisfy the current academic, enrollment, and other program requirements.
  • Texas Educational Opportunity Grant (TEOG): state grant support for eligible students at participating community colleges, public state colleges, and public technical institutes.
  • Tuition Equalization Grant (TEG): grant support for eligible students attending participating private or independent colleges and universities in Texas.

These are state financial-aid programs, not a complete list of private scholarships. The programs have distinct requirements and eligible institutions. Texas residency alone does not make every student eligible. Check the current program rules and ask the financial-aid office at the school you plan to attend.

Search for local Texas scholarships

Local awards can be missed when students rely only on national scholarship directories. Search by county, city, high school, community foundation, intended college, and academic department. Ask your counselor about scholarships funded by local businesses, civic clubs, service organizations, and donors who support graduates from particular schools.

Try searches such as your county plus “community foundation scholarship,” your high school plus “scholarship,” or your intended college plus “departmental scholarships.” Follow every result to the sponsor's current page; a directory listing may be out of date.

Ask which financial-aid application is right for you

FAFSA is the federal financial-aid application. Texas also has TASFA, an application route used by some students who are not eligible to complete FAFSA and may be considered for certain state aid programs. The right application depends on your circumstances and the program. Contact the financial-aid office at the institution you plan to attend before choosing an application route.

Build a Texas-focused scholarship tracker

  1. Record the Texas colleges or eligible institutions you are considering.
  2. List awards tied to your county, high school, major, activities, or intended college.
  3. Keep grants, scholarships, work-study, and loans in separate categories so you can compare them accurately.
  4. Record the official sponsor URL, deadline, enrollment conditions, residency rules, GPA criteria, and required documents.
  5. Recheck the sponsor's current page before applying; program rules and funding can change each award year.

How Scholarship Scout can support a Texas search

Scholarship Scout supports state-based scholarship discovery and organization. A student can select Texas, an expected graduation year, and relevant categories to find public research leads. The app can also help organize opportunities, compare sponsor criteria using information kept in the student's private workspace, and track application deadlines.

The public discovery request is limited to state, expected graduation year, and selected category IDs. GPA, essays, school details, and personal notes are not sent in that request. Search results still need to be checked against official sponsor rules; the tool does not guarantee eligibility or funding.
